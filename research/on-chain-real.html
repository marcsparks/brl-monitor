The on-chain real, measured for the first time

BRL Monitor Research · August 2026 · every figure links to its source

Brazil's currency has quietly developed a second body. Alongside the real in your bank account, there is now a real that lives on public blockchains — six tokens, issued by a bank, a Paradigm-backed startup, two ramp companies, an exchange consortium and a DeFi protocol. Until now, nobody counted it. This site exists to count it, and this essay is the first reading.

US$166 million of BRL-pegged tokens circulate on public chains today, across six issuers and six blockchains — every unit individually visible on-chain. Twelve months ago the comparable figure was roughly a third of that.

The seven-month coup

The story of the past year is Crown's BRLV. Launched around December 2025 with backing from Paradigm's first-ever Brazil investment, it is not a payments stablecoin but a yield-sharing token backed by Brazilian government bonds — the country's ~14% policy rate, wrapped in an ERC-20. In seven months it went from zero to the largest BRL token on any blockchain (≈R$390M, verify on-chain), overtaking Transfero's BRZ, which had led the category since 2019.

The holder data tells you who is buying. BRLV has fewer than a hundred holders — family offices and institutions entering the famous Brazilian carry trade through a token instead of a brokerage account. At the other pole, Avenia's BRLA has over nineteen thousand holders — retail-scale distribution through apps and ramps. Same peg, opposite markets: one token is a bond desk, the other is plumbing.

The measured market behind it

The on-chain real is the small, perfectly-visible tip of something much larger and officially measured. Brazil's central bank now reports that companies bought US$14.7 billion of stablecoins abroad in the first half of 2026 — more than double a year earlier (BCB external-sector statistics). The tax authority's mandatory reporting shows USDT alone accounts for ~90% of domestic stablecoin operations. No other country publishes this level of official measurement — Brazil is, by law, the best-measured stablecoin market in the world. What was missing was a public interface to that measurement, plus the on-chain layer officials don't track. That combination is this site.

What happens on October 30

Under the central bank's new crypto framework (Resolutions 519/520/521), every company providing crypto services in Brazil must file for authorization by October 30, 2026 — and non-filers must wind down within 30 days. When the authorized list publishes, a market that has operated on reputation becomes a public census: who is licensed, who filed, and who has been visibly operating without either. Our license tracker is counting down; the day the register populates, this page's sequel writes itself.

Method, in one paragraph

Every number on the BRL Monitor dashboard is either complete by construction — read directly from verified token contracts, where a blockchain records every mint, burn, transfer and holder — or complete by law, taken from mandatory official statistics (BCB, Receita, CVM) and linked to the publication. Where a figure can't meet that bar, the dashboard shows a dash, and the analysis moves here, to Research, with its assumptions in the open. The pipeline is open source and runs twice a day.
